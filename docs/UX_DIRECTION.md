# NK Bag House — UX and Visual Direction

## 1. Experience objective

Create a polished, confident, easy-to-use B2B website that presents NK Bag House as a capable bulk-bag manufacturing business. The experience should feel premium and considered without becoming visually crowded, overly minimal, or difficult to navigate.

The intended quality bar is inspired by the presentation discipline of established retail brands such as Peter England and Allen Solly: strong visual hierarchy, curated imagery, well-organised discovery, consistent product presentation, and clear actions. These are references for quality and usability only. Do not copy their logos, layouts, copy, photography, icons, or other protected brand assets.

## 2. What "premium but user-friendly" means

- A strong first impression with one clear headline, one supporting message, and a primary call to action.
- Large, authentic product and manufacturing photography with consistent art direction.
- Clear navigation and obvious paths to products, custom requirements, and contact.
- Generous but purposeful spacing; avoid both cramped sections and excessive empty space.
- Consistent typography, image ratios, button styles, card layouts, and interaction states.
- Useful product information presented in scannable sections rather than dense paragraphs.
- Restrained animation that supports orientation or feedback instead of distracting visitors.
- A consistent, accessible experience on phones, tablets, and desktops.

Premium does not mean adding a carousel, pop-up, mega menu, account system, or animation to every page. Add a component only when it improves a real customer task.

## 3. Proposed information architecture

Primary navigation proposal:

- Home
- Products
- Custom Solutions
- About
- Contact
- **Request a Quote** (visually prominent action)

A "Manufacturing & Quality" page or navigation item may be added only when the business has verified information and approved images to support it.

Use a category mega menu only if the confirmed catalogue has enough categories to justify it. Otherwise, use a clean dropdown or direct catalogue link. Add catalogue search and filters only when the number of products makes browsing difficult without them.

### Product families and catalogue navigation

The catalogue should clearly separate the business's communicated product groups:

**Manufactured bag categories**
- School Bags
- Travel Bags
- General Bags

**Leather product categories**
- Leather Bags
- Leather Wallets
- Leather Keychains
- Leather Card Holders
- Leather Belts

Show these as distinct, easy-to-scan category groups on the Products page and, where appropriate, as visual category cards on the home page. A visitor should be able to open a category and browse the products listed under it. Do not create individual product listings, variants, or specifications until the business supplies the actual catalogue and approved images.

The business has also expressed an affordable-price and quality-focused positioning. Use careful wording such as "Affordable options" or "Discuss your budget and requirements" until specific price points, material standards, quality processes, and sourcing arrangements are confirmed. Confirm which leather products are manufactured by NK Bag House and which, if any, are sourced or supplied through partners before describing their origin.

## 4. Homepage content sequence

1. **Header** — logo, concise navigation, and a clear quote/enquiry action.
2. **Hero** — authentic, high-quality product or manufacturing image; concise value proposition; primary "Request a Quote" action and a secondary "Explore Products" action.
3. **Product categories** — visually distinct cards for School Bags, Travel Bags, General Bags, and a separate Leather Products group.
4. **Business capabilities** — verified order range, customisation options, or production capabilities. Do not publish unverified capacity or quality claims.
5. **Featured products** — a curated selection linking to detailed product information.
6. **How enquiries work** — a simple explanation of how customers share requirements and receive a response.
7. **Trust and proof** — only approved certifications, real testimonials, customer logos, facility images, or factual business information.
8. **Final enquiry callout** — make the next step obvious.
9. **Footer** — contact details, page links, business information, privacy information, and copyright notice.

The exact section order can change after the customer supplies the UI references and confirms the business content.

## 5. Product catalogue and detail experience

### Catalogue

- Use a responsive grid with consistent image aspect ratios.
- Make category names and product titles easy to scan.
- Keep cards visually calm: image, product name, short factual descriptor, and a clear "View details" or "Enquire" action.
- Use filters only for attributes that customers actually need and that the business can maintain accurately.
- Avoid displaying a price if the business has not approved a public pricing policy.

### Product detail

- Give the product image enough visual prominence.
- Present verified specifications in a structured table or labelled groups.
- Explain available options and customisation without overwhelming the page.
- Keep the enquiry action visible near the product summary and again after specifications.
- Carry the selected product into the enquiry form so customers do not need to re-enter it.

## 6. Enquiry experience

The enquiry path is the primary conversion journey.

- Use a short, clearly labelled form with only necessary fields.
- Ask for the requested quantity and a useful description of the requirement.
- Separate required fields from optional information.
- Explain why a field is requested when that is not obvious.
- Make validation messages specific and place them near the relevant field.
- Preserve entered values after recoverable errors.
- Show a confirmation only after the server confirms that the enquiry was accepted.
- Provide a clear alternative contact method once the business approves the details.
- Make the form comfortable to complete on a mobile device.

## 7. Responsive behaviour

### Mobile

- Compact header with an accessible menu.
- Single-column content where needed and large tap targets.
- Product cards that remain readable without tiny text.
- Full-width or comfortably sized enquiry controls.
- A prominent enquiry action without covering important content.
- Avoid wide specification tables; use stacked labelled rows when appropriate.

### Tablet

- Use intermediate grid layouts and preserve comfortable spacing.
- Avoid simply stretching the mobile layout to fill the screen.

### Desktop

- Use a deliberate content max-width and consistent alignment.
- Use multi-column product grids where image and text sizes remain legible.
- Allow larger editorial imagery and structured product details.
- Keep navigation and enquiry actions easy to locate.

## 8. Visual inspiration guardrails

Translate the premium-retail reference into a manufacturing-business context:

- Use campaign-style imagery to tell the story of products and manufacturing.
- Use category-led discovery rather than fashion-style promotions.
- Use clear product families, technical details, and enquiry actions.
- Replace retail discount banners, shopping carts, wishlists, and fashion filters with useful B2B information.
- Never imply that NK Bag House offers a feature, certification, delivery promise, or service unless the business confirms it.

## 9. Success criteria

The UI direction is successful when a first-time visitor can quickly:

1. Understand what NK Bag House manufactures.
2. Find the relevant product or category.
3. Understand the key verified specifications.
4. Submit a bulk or custom enquiry without confusion.
5. Find the business's approved contact details.

## 10. Pending customer inputs

- Final logo and approved brand assets.
- UI screenshots or reference URLs selected by the customer.
- Confirmed product catalogue under School Bags, Travel Bags, General Bags, and Leather Products (Leather Bags, Wallets, Keychains, Card Holders, and Belts), plus approved product photography.
- Confirm which product lines are manufactured in-house and which are sourced or supplied, if applicable.
- Approved specifications and capability statements.
- Final contact details and enquiry recipient.
- Brand colour and typography preferences, if already established.

Do not treat the design direction as final until the customer reviews the proposed visual system.
